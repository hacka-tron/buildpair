Everything lives in a space: a list of to-dos, habits and goals, with one chat for the people in it. "Life" might be shared with your partner, "Ledgerly" with fellow builders, "Running" with a friend, and "Private" with nobody.

Drawn iPhone-style in the Margin Notes direction. Android uses the same layout with its own native controls, and the last screen shows the laptop version.

1. Today, grouped by space

The daily to-do list, grouped by the space each thing lives in, so you can see who's along for each part of your day.

  • The floating + in the bottom-right corner, like TickTick's, opens a small add bar that floats above the keyboard, over a dimmed Today screen, so you never lose your place (second phone). Type what it is, and natural language still works: "dentist friday 3pm" fills in the date for you.
  • Under the text are two chips. The date chip opens the scheduler (third phone): quick picks (Today, Tomorrow, Friday, Next week), a small calendar, time, reminder and repeat.
  • The space chip opens a pull-up list of your spaces (screen 4), so the item lands in the right place. It starts on your most recent space.
  • No "habit" or "goal" buttons. The schedule decides: a single date makes a to-do; setting it to repeat (fourth phone, "4 times a week") makes a habit with weekly progress; a deadline with steps underneath becomes a goal.
  • Ledgerly (shared with four fellow builders) holds work goals. The Jira bug ticked itself off.
  • Life (shared with Jordan and 3 friends) holds one-off things like picking up the kids, plus the gym habit with its four weekly dots.
  • Private is for things nobody else sees.
  • Each space's section collapses with the arrow next to its name, so you can fold away what you don't need right now.
  • Tabs: Today, Spaces (all of yours), Supporting (spaces others share with you), Discover. Your profile is the photo in the top corner, next to the bell.

2. Spaces: everything you've set up

The second tab lists your own spaces, most recent activity first.

  • Each row shows the space's name, a short summary (goals, habits, what's left this week), and the faces of the people in it.
  • A blue dot means new activity in the chat. Requests and On Discover tags show when people have offered support, or when the space is visible on Discover.
  • On this tab, the floating + creates a new space and opens its setup (screen 4). Old spaces can be archived and live at the bottom.
  • Tap a row to open the space (next screen).

3. Inside a space: the list and the chat together

Opening a space shows what's in it and the conversation about it on one screen.

  • On top, the list: habits, goals and to-dos, each labeled. The list and the chat each collapse with their arrow. Finished things fold into "Done this week" instead of cluttering the list.
  • Below, the chat: everything that happens in the space, plus messages.
  • Small to-dos roll up into one line per day ("Maya finished 4 things in Life") so the chat stays readable. Goals and habits post their own lines.
  • Reactions stack, like in Teams or Slack: five people sending 🔥 shows as one "🔥 5" chip. Tap a chip to add yours; press and hold to see who reacted (shown on the left phone).
  • Done is one tap: tick the circle and you're finished. No forms.
  • Long-press an item to select it, like in TickTick (middle). The bottom bar turns into an action bar: Skip today, Won't do, Update (post a note to the chat) and Move. Tap more items to act on several at once.
  • The ••• at the far right of the action bar opens the full menu (right): edit, change schedule, reminder, move to another space, only visible to me, duplicate, delete.
  • Skip and Won't do apply instantly. A small bar then offers Add a reason, which you can tap or ignore.
  • Update lets you post a note about any item at any time ("Halfway through the plan, feeling good"), and it lands in the space's chat. That's how "Car broke down. Going Saturday instead." got there, and why Jordan could offer to take the kids.
  • Any single item can be set to Only visible to me from the full menu, and it stays private inside a shared space.

4. Picking a space, and creating one

The pull-up you get from the space chip when adding anything (left). Tap a space and you're done.

  • An existing space, so the people already in it see the new item too. "First 10 paying users" would go into Ledgerly.
  • A new space: tap the New space row at the bottom of the list (it has a + where the others have a circle) to open a short setup (right). Name it and say what it's for (required, because that's what supporters read). Who's in it: add people by name, username or ID, or share an invite link. Flip Show on Discover to let new people offer support, and optionally add anything else you'd like from them. The same controls live on every space's people screen. The space gets an automatic color and initial; tap the icon if you want to change it.
  • Private if nobody should see it.
  • Anything can move between spaces later, from its long-press menu.

5. Who's in a space

Sharing is set per space, not per goal, so it's one decision instead of many.

  • Everyone is listed as a person. Add friends by invite, or post the space to Discover so new people can offer support. A space with several people in it is the group; there's no separate "group" to manage.
  • Everyone in a space sees its list and chat, reacts and messages.
  • Adding people: start typing in Add people (screen 4 shows it open). Suggestions come first from people you already share spaces with, then anyone by name or username. You can also add someone by their Buildpair ID (like BP-4821-KQ), which everyone has on their profile. Everyone added shows in the list with their role (Supporter, or remove them from the same menu).
  • Invite with a link, the way group chats like WhatsApp or Discord do it: tap Share to send the link by text, email or any app. Anyone who opens it can ask to join, and their request lands in the space's Requests for you to approve. You can reset the link anytime to stop old invites working.
  • The same controls appear in the new-space setup, along with the Show on Discover switch.
  • Exceptions: any single item can be set to "Only visible to me" from its long-press menu. It shows a small lock in the list and stays private even inside a shared space.
  • A space with nobody else in it is simply private.

6. Supporting: spaces others share with you

The same idea from the other side.

  • Tap Check in to open the space's chat (right). Suggestions based on their last update sit above the message box. Tap one to start a message you can edit.
  • The bell opens Activity: reactions, replies and requests aimed at you.

7. Discover: spaces that want support

No separate post to write. A space appears on Discover when you flip its Show on Discover switch (screens 4 and 5).

  • What people see (first phone) comes from the space itself, plus the owner's top badge and compliment: what it's for, its goals, and anything extra you asked for ("Also wants: train together"). Locked items stay hidden. Flip the switch off to take it down.
  • For you shows spaces that fit you, with reasons: a similar goal, overlapping hours, same time zone. Browse lets you filter yourself.
  • Offer support (second phone): either just support their space, or support each other, where you suggest one or more of your spaces they could support back. Add a short note.
  • Requests land in your space (third phone): who's offering, their note, and for "support each other", the spaces they suggested and what each needs. You pick one of those spaces, then accept or decline.
  • Keeping it healthy: limited open offers, offers expire after a week, and anyone can decline, leave or block quietly. No swiping.

8. Profile and settings

Neither needs a tab. Your photo in the top corner of Today opens your profile, and the gear on your profile opens settings.

  • Profile (left): photo, name, username, your Buildpair ID (tap to copy, so friends can add you), time zone, a line about what you're building, and a plain line of how you support others.
  • Badges are earned automatically from what you actually do, so they can be trusted: Fast responder (usually replies within a day), Reliable supporter (checked in on someone for 4+ weeks), Finisher (finished 5 goals), Season complete, and a few more.
  • Compliments come from the people you support, and they add up ("Honest takes 12"). They're positive only.
  • Giving one (right): after a few weeks of support, or when a goal is finished, the app asks "How's Dana as a supporter?" Pick any compliments and add an optional note. There are no star ratings, which tend to inflate and make people hold back honest feedback. If it isn't working, "Not a good fit" quietly improves future matches, and Report handles anything serious.
  • On Discover: the spaces you've posted and how many offers are waiting.
  • Finished goals you chose to make public, which is what other people see when deciding whether to offer support.
  • Settings (middle), in native grouped lists: notifications (daily reminder, weekly reflection, quiet hours), privacy (where new items go, who sees your profile, blocked people), connected tools (Jira; more tools on Pro), appearance (follow the system's light or dark mode; the extra themes live here, on Pro), and account (plan, email, help, log out).

9. Where the Buildpair brand shows up

Native apps keep branding out of the way inside the product. The brand belongs where people first meet the app, or meet it outside the app.

  • The mark: two rounded shapes side by side, one filled royal blue and one outlined. It reads as "you and the person in your corner" without spelling it out. On the royal blue app icon both shapes turn white.
  • App icon on the home screen, as an iPhone rounded square and an Android adaptive icon, plus the browser tab icon on the web.
  • Launch screen (first phone): just the mark on white for the split second the app opens. No slogan, no animation.
  • Welcome screen (second phone): the wordmark, a short promise ("Set goals. Bring people along."), and sign-up. Invite links skip straight into the space after sign-up.
  • Notifications (third phone): the app icon is the brand. The text is about people ("Jordan in Life"), not about Buildpair.
  • Invite links (fourth phone): when you share a space, messaging apps show a preview card with the mark on royal blue, the space name and what it's for. This is how most new people will first see Buildpair, so it's worth getting right.
  • Web: the wordmark at the top of the sidebar (screen 10), and on the landing page and emails (not drawn).
  • Not inside the app's screens: Today, Spaces, Supporting and Discover don't carry a logo in their headers. Royal blue on buttons, notes and selection already does that job.
  • This mark is a starting point. Before launch, check the name and mark for conflicts (a trademark search), and consider having a designer refine it.

What changed

Sharing now happens per space instead of per goal. One-off to-dos finally have a home ("Life"). Big goals can get their own space or join an existing one, and every space has one chat with small to-dos rolled up so it stays readable.

Not drawn yet: the Activity screen behind the bell, habits in detail, the shared focus timer, and the Pro upgrade screen.
